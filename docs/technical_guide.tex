\noindent This guide documents every analytical stage of the MDBC study: what each step
does, why it was designed that way, what it produced, and what alternatives were considered
and rejected. It is an onboarding reference: paired with the executable notebook
(\file{MDBC\_pipeline.ipynb}) and the pipeline runner (\file{run\_all.sh}), it should let a
new contributor reproduce, audit, and extend the work. Code lives in \file{src/}; derived
artifacts in \file{data/processed/}; figures are built by \file{src/manuscript\_assets.py}
and \file{figures\_d3/}.

\section{Overview and design philosophy}
The study asks one operational question: \emph{can a global blue-carbon model predict the
carbon stock of a delta it has never seen?} The entire pipeline is built to answer that
honestly, which means three commitments that recur throughout:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The held-out unit is a whole geographic system}, not a random point, because
  that is the situation a carbon project or a new coastline actually faces.
  \item \textbf{Metrics are reported honestly}: out-of-region we report the within-region
  correlation (pattern) and bias (level) separately, and area-of-applicability coverage,
  because a single $R^2$ confounds distinct failures and is dominated by small folds.
  \item \textbf{Every strong claim is stress-tested}: an ablation, a validity check, a
  threshold sweep, a second carbon pool, a published product, and a scale test on 29 regions.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Data flow.} \file{raw (public sources) $\to$ labels $\to$ covariate join $\to$
models $\to$ results JSON $\to$ figures/tables}. The raster inputs ($\sim$8.5\,GB) are
referenced in place via \file{MDBC\_LAKE} and never redistributed; the small derived tables
and all results are tracked and archived (Zenodo \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20787021}{10.5281/zenodo.20787021}).

\section{Stage 0 --- Data sources and the delta registry}
\textbf{Code:} \file{src/acquire/*}, \file{src/build\_region\_folds.py}, \file{config/deltas.yaml}.\\
\textbf{Sources:} Coastal Carbon Network (SOC cores), Global Mangrove Watch v3 (extent),
Simard 2019 (AGB), EOT20 (tides), CHELSA/SoilGrids/GSOCmap/Copernicus (covariates),
Natural Earth (geomorphic), WoSIS (terrestrial control).

\why The leave-one-delta-out folds must be defined before modelling and by transparent
rules, so they cannot be accused of cherry-picking. A system becomes a soil-carbon fold if
it has sufficient mangrove area (GMW-clipped, equal-area), at least five \emph{usable} cores,
and a non-overlapping window (no pixel shared between train and test). We also require the
fold set to span $\geq 4$ continents.

\out Eight named core deltas across four continents; seven sizeable ``prediction-only''
deltas with no usable cores (used later for the crediting argument).

\alt (i) Country units --- ecologically meaningless. (ii) Random points --- the inflated
baseline we critique. (iii) Raw grid cells --- adopted later as the 29-region robustness check.

\section{Stage 1 --- Labels and covariates}
\subsection{Soil organic carbon labels}
\textbf{Code:} \file{src/labels/build\_soc\_labels.py}.
Each CCN mangrove core's depth series is integrated to a standardized 0--100\,cm stock,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SOC}_{0\text{--}100}=100\sum_i \mathrm{BD}_i\,f_{C,i}\,\Delta z_i\quad[\si{\mega\gram\per\hectare}],
\end{equation}
with $f_C$ from measured carbon fraction or, where absent, $0.427\times$ loss-on-ignition
organic matter (the median ratio over $\sim$12k paired samples). Layers outside physical
bounds are dropped; cores must reach 80\,cm; the gap to 100\,cm is filled with the deepest
density and flagged.

\out 2{,}489 usable cores (median 278\,\si{\mega\gram\per\hectare}). Per-delta medians span
$\sim$60--520 while mean annual temperature is near-uniform --- the first hint that density is
set by non-climatic, delta-specific factors.

\alt Using a predicted SOC map as the target would be circular; concentration instead of
stock is reserved for the terrestrial control (where bulk density is the limiting measurement).

\subsection{Covariates}
\textbf{Code:} \file{src/features/sample\_covariates.py}, \file{build\_geomorphic.py},
\file{build\_tidal.py}. Climate (CHELSA bio1--19), a soil-carbon prior (GSOCmap, used
cautiously), soil texture (SoilGrids), land cover (Copernicus), geomorphic position
(distance-to-coast/-river from Natural Earth), and tidal forcing (mean/spring range and form
factor from the EOT20 M2/S2/K1/O1 constituents). Each layer is sampled in its native CRS with
per-layer nodata handling; corrupt lake layers are tolerated and logged.

\alt FES2014 tides (credential-blocked) were replaced with the open EOT20; OpenLandMap SOC was
dropped as truncated and circular.

\section{Stage 2 --- The transferability experiment}
\subsection{Three-tier validation}
\textbf{Code:} \file{src/models/soc\_lodo.py}. Two estimators (ridge; histogram
gradient-boosting) under: \textbf{T1} random $k$-fold; \textbf{T2} spatial-block CV
($5^\circ$ blocks, Roberts 2017); \textbf{T3} leave-one-delta-out.

\out T1 $R^2=0.65 \to$ T2 $-0.69 \to$ T3 median $-1.71$. The scale-invariant truth: median
within-delta Pearson $r\approx0.04$ out-of-region, with per-delta bias $\pm$100--200; the
model recovers neither level nor pattern of an unsampled delta. The collapse is model-agnostic.

\alt Reporting only random CV (the inflated norm); reporting only $R^2$ (confounds level and
pattern, dominated by tiny folds like Sundarbans $n=11$). We add $r$ and bias and lead with
the median.

\subsection{Area of applicability and conformal intervals}
\textbf{Code:} \file{soc\_lodo.aoa\_di}. Importance-weighted dissimilarity index (Meyer \&
Pebesma 2021) with the CAST boxplot-whisker threshold (Q75$+1.5\,$IQR). Split-conformal
intervals (target 90\%) on held-out calibration residuals.

\out Every held-out delta is \textbf{0\% inside the AOA}. Conformal coverage degrades under
shift and is sample-size dependent (Amazon $n{=}69$: 90\%; Sundarbans $n{=}11$: 0\%).

\subsection{Scale robustness: 29 regions}
\textbf{Code:} \file{src/build\_region\_folds.py} (DBSCAN, 250\,km), \file{src/models/region\_lodo.py}.

\why To defeat the ``$n{=}8$ is too few'' objection without naming deltas.

\out 29 data-driven regions, 6 continents; median within-region $r=-0.09$, every region
outside the AOA, 90\% with negative $R^2$. Here we lead with $r$ and AOA because in-sample
$R^2$ is lower on the full heterogeneous core set ($0.10$ vs $0.65$), so the $R^2$ gap is the
wrong headline.

\subsection{Mechanism: covariate vs concept shift}
\textbf{Code:} \file{transfer\_diagnostic.py}, \file{concept\_shift.py}.

\out (i) Covariate-shift magnitude does \emph{not} order the per-delta failures (Spearman
weak, $n{=}8$ underpowered) --- distance alone is not the story. (ii) Concept shift, directly:
allowing per-delta regression slopes beats shared slopes under within-delta CV ($R^2\,0.43\to0.67$)
and the slopes flip sign across deltas. With five deltas this is suggestive, not conclusive.

\alt Stopping at ``it fails''; or a full domain-adaptation formalism. The slope test is the
minimal transparent demonstration.

\subsection{Robustness suite}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{GSOC ablation} (\file{gsoc\_ablation.py}): removing the circular prior changes
  random-CV $R^2$ by $<0.005$ and LODO by $<0.2$ --- the gap is not manufactured by it.
  \item \textbf{AOA validity} (\file{aoa\_validity.py}): random points from \emph{seen} deltas
  are 96\% inside the AOA; spatial-block and whole-delta holdouts give 0\% --- AOA discriminates
  familiar from novel, so $0\%$ is a finding not a tautology.
  \item \textbf{AOA threshold sensitivity} (\file{aoa\_threshold\_sensitivity.py}): inside-fraction
  stays 0 from $0.5\times$ to $2\times$ the standard threshold.
  \item \textbf{Published-map test} (\file{published\_map\_test.py}): the Sanderman 2018 product,
  streamed via \texttt{/vsicurl/} and tested against in-situ cores, shows median within-delta
  $r\approx-0.11$ and median $|\text{bias}|\approx116$ --- the failure extends to a product in use.
  \emph{Caveat:} Sanderman was trained on overlapping cores and predates many in epoch, so its
  agreement here is optimistic; that it still fails per delta strengthens the point.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Few-shot calibration}
\textbf{Code:} \file{fewshot\_calibration.py}. Add $k$ target-delta cores to training; also a
\emph{local-only} baseline ($k$ cores without the global model).

\out global$+$local recovers within-delta $r$: $0.14\to0.55$ ($k{=}10$) $\to0.67$ ($k{=}25$);
but $k$ local cores \emph{alone} give $r\approx0$. Neither global-alone nor local-alone works
--- only the combination. Global and local data are \textbf{complementary, not substitutable}.

\alt Showing only the rising global$+$local curve (reads as trivial ``data helps''); the
local-only baseline is what makes it non-trivial. Deep transfer learning is overkill for
hundreds of cores.

\section{Stage 3 --- AGB, total carbon, terrestrial control, crediting}
\subsection{Aboveground biomass and total carbon}
\textbf{Code:} \file{build\_agb\_labels.py}, \file{agb\_lodo.py}, \file{total\_carbon.py}.
AGB-C (Simard$\times0.47$) reproduces the collapse (random $0.64\to$ LODO median $-0.60$,
AOA$=0$): two pools, same failure. Total ecosystem carbon is \SI{495}{\tera\gram}\,C across
eight deltas (95\% CI 460--516), 77\% belowground, densities spanning $6\times$.
\emph{Caveat:} Simard embeds a climate-dependent allometry, so AGB-from-climate transfer is
partly circular --- a companion to, not an independent replicate of, the SOC result.

\subsection{Terrestrial specificity control}
\textbf{Code:} \file{terrestrial\_test.py}. WoSIS 0--30\,cm SOC \emph{concentration} (no bulk
density needed $\Rightarrow$ 133k profiles, 37\% North America, 6 balanced continents),
leave-one-continent-out.

\out Median within-region $r=0.54$ (positive on every continent) and LOCO $R^2=+0.23$ ---
terrestrial soil carbon transfers \emph{far} better, so the blue-carbon failure is specific,
not a generic mapping artifact. (GSOCmap is a secondary check on the smaller, US-heavy
bulk-density subset.)

\alt The original stock-based version was 93\% North American; switching to concentration
fixed the geographic balance and \emph{strengthened} the contrast.

\subsection{Carbon-crediting stakes}
\textbf{Code:} \file{crediting\_risk.py}. The seven cored-free deltas are 8\% of global
mangrove area. Because density does not transfer (the paper's own finding), a global model
cannot pin their stock better than the observed $6\times$ density spread:
$\sim$0.5--2.7\,\si{\peta\gram}\,CO$_2$e --- an order-of-magnitude span no current product
reports. This is framed as un-knowability, not a point estimate, to avoid assuming the very
transfer we disprove.

\section{Reproducibility and extension}
\textbf{Reproduce:} \file{pip install -r requirements.txt}; \file{export MDBC\_LAKE=...} (or
\file{./fetch\_lake.sh}); \file{./run\_all.sh}. \textbf{Notebook:} \file{docs/MDBC\_pipeline.ipynb}
runs from the tracked derived data in minutes. \textbf{Figures:} \file{src/manuscript\_assets.py}
then \file{figures\_d3/build.sh}.

\paragraph{Natural extensions.} (i) Per-pixel total-carbon mapping with co-located AGB+SOC
and jointly propagated uncertainty. (ii) A hierarchical / partial-pooling model that admits
delta-specific structure, tested against the few-shot benchmark. (iii) The saltmarsh and
seagrass pools (cores already on disk) for a multi-ecosystem coastal-carbon version. (iv) A
formal MRV-cost analysis turning the density span into a credit-risk figure.
