\textbf{Re: Submission of an original research manuscript}

Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, \textbf{``Cross-delta transferability limits of
mangrove blue-carbon stock models: a multi-delta benchmark with area-of-applicability and
conformal uncertainty,''} for consideration as an original research article in
\textit{Communications Earth \& Environment}.

Global maps of mangrove soil and biomass carbon are now used to set baselines for
blue-carbon credits and to prioritise conservation, yet their accuracy is validated
almost exclusively with random or spatially-pooled cross-validation. Such validation is
inflated by spatial autocorrelation and is silent on the question that actually matters
for a carbon buyer or a newly-surveyed coastline: can a model trained on some deltas
predict an \emph{unsampled} one? We pose and answer this question with a reproducible
multi-delta benchmark spanning eight major mangrove systems across four continents.

We believe the study fits the scope of \textit{Communications Earth \& Environment} for
three reasons:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A quantified, operationally relevant limit.} Under leave-one-delta-out
  evaluation a model scoring $R^2=0.65$ in random cross-validation captures essentially
  no within-delta variation (median within-delta correlation $r\approx0$) and is biased
  by 100--200~Mg\,ha$^{-1}$; every held-out delta falls entirely outside the model's area
  of applicability. The same per-delta failure appears when we test a widely-used
  published soil-carbon product directly against in-situ cores, so the caution applies to
  maps already in use, not only to our re-implementation.
  \item \textbf{A mechanism and a constructive remedy.} We show the failure is driven by
  concept shift --- the environment--carbon relationship itself differs between deltas,
  with covariate slopes that reverse sign --- rather than by missing predictors, which
  adding tidal and geomorphic forcing does not remedy. We then quantify the data cost of
  fixing it: on the order of ten to twenty-five local cores recover most of the lost
  within-delta skill, defining a practical middle path between an untrustworthy global
  prediction and a full local inventory.
  \item \textbf{A reusable, uncertainty-aware framework.} We release a frozen multi-delta
  benchmark with leave-one-delta-out splits, a three-tier validation protocol, and
  area-of-applicability and conformal-prediction layers that tell a practitioner where a
  global model can be trusted --- portable to any coastal system by a bounding box.
\end{itemize}

We have been deliberately conservative in interpretation. We report median alongside mean
skill because small folds dominate the latter; we separate level error from pattern error
rather than leaning on a single composite metric; and we state explicitly the limitations
of sparse in-situ labels, the partial circularity of the biomass product, and the fact
that version~1 is soil-carbon-led. The complete pipeline, pinned environment, and all
derived data are openly archived to ensure full reproducibility.

\textbf{Declarations.} This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and
is not under consideration elsewhere. The study involved no human participants or animals.
All primary datasets are public, and our code and derived data are archived to ensure
reproducibility. The author declares no competing interests.

Suitable reviewers may be drawn from the fields of blue-carbon biogeochemistry, spatial
machine learning for ecological mapping, and mangrove remote sensing; we are happy to
provide names on request.

Thank you for considering our manuscript. We look forward to your response.
