\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Mangroves are among Earth's most carbon-dense ecosystems, and global maps of
their soil and biomass carbon now underpin carbon-crediting and conservation
decisions. Yet these maps are validated almost exclusively with random or
in-distribution cross-validation, which is inflated by spatial autocorrelation
and silent about whether a model trained on some regions can predict an
\emph{unsampled} delta. Here we build a multi-delta benchmark spanning eight major
mangrove systems across four continents and evaluate both soil organic carbon (SOC)
and aboveground biomass carbon (AGB-C) under three validation tiers --- random,
spatial-block, and leave-one-delta-out (LODO) --- coupled with area-of-applicability
(AOA) masking and distribution-free conformal prediction intervals. A gradient-boosting
model scoring $R^2=0.65$ under random cross-validation collapses to a median LODO $R^2$
of $-1.7$, every held-out delta falls entirely outside the AOA, and adding geomorphic
position and tidal forcing does not restore transfer. The same collapse occurs
independently for AGB-C, and both pools place every delta outside the AOA. The failure
is not explained by covariate-shift magnitude alone, implicating delta-specific
(concept-level) controls on carbon density, and conformal intervals lose their coverage
guarantee under cross-delta shift. Across the benchmark, total ecosystem carbon (495~Tg
C; 77\% belowground) varies sixfold between deltas in a way a global model cannot
reconstruct. Our results quantify a hard limit on the geographic transferability of
blue-carbon stock models and argue that crediting-grade estimates for an unsampled
delta require local calibration data.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} blue carbon; mangroves; soil organic carbon;
out-of-distribution generalization; area of applicability; conformal prediction;
spatial cross-validation.

\section{Introduction}
Mangroves are among the most carbon-dense ecosystems on Earth, storing of order
11.7~Pg of carbon globally, the large majority of it---roughly 10~Pg---belowground in
waterlogged, slowly decomposing soils \citep{kauffman2020}. This soil-dominated stock
makes mangroves disproportionately valuable for climate mitigation and has driven a
rapid expansion of wall-to-wall carbon products: a 30~m global map of mangrove soil
carbon \citep{sanderman2018}, a 30~m map of aboveground biomass and canopy height
\citep{simard2019}, and continental syntheses of stock change and loss-driven
emissions \citep{richards2020netloss,adame2021future}. These products increasingly
underpin operational decisions---setting baselines for blue-carbon credits, targeting
restoration, and informing national plans such as the Bangladesh Delta Plan 2100.

The credibility of those decisions rests on how well the underlying models predict
where they have not been trained, yet this is precisely what conventional validation
does not test. Global blue-carbon maps are almost universally assessed with random or
spatially-pooled cross-validation, which is inflated by spatial autocorrelation:
\citet{ploton2020} showed that for tropical forest biomass a model appearing to
explain more than half of the variance had near-zero skill once spatial dependence was
respected. Mangrove carbon maps have not been stress-tested this way, and the
operationally decisive question---can soil-carbon knowledge learned in some deltas
predict an \emph{unsampled} delta, the situation a carbon buyer or a newly-surveyed
coastline actually faces---has not been posed. Answering it requires both an
out-of-distribution evaluation and a way to say, for any location, whether a global
model is even applicable there \citep{meyer2021}, with calibrated uncertainty that is
honest under distribution shift.

Here we address this gap with a multi-delta benchmark and an explicitly
transferability-focused evaluation. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A reproducible \emph{multi-delta benchmark} with frozen leave-one-delta-out
        splits for mangrove total ecosystem carbon (AGB-C + SOC).
  \item Three-tier validation that exposes the spatial-autocorrelation inflation
        and quantifies the \emph{transfer gap}.
  \item Trustworthiness layers --- area of applicability \citep{meyer2021} and
        conformal intervals --- that tell a practitioner \emph{where} a global
        model may be used.
  \item A mechanistic diagnostic separating covariate shift from concept shift.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Results}

\subsection{A multi-delta blue-carbon benchmark}
Applying the pre-registered selection criteria (Methods) to the candidate windows
yielded, for each system, its Global Mangrove Watch mangrove area and its count of
usable in-situ soil cores; systems meeting both the area and label thresholds became
soil-carbon LODO folds, those meeting only the area threshold became prediction-only
deltas, and the remainder were excluded. The v1 SOC benchmark comprises eight core
deltas spanning four continents (Table~\ref{tab:deltas}, Fig.~\ref{fig:map}); median
0--100~cm SOC stocks range from
\SI{61}{\mega\gram\per\hectare} (Zambezi) to \SI{519}{\mega\gram\per\hectare}
(Musi--Banyuasin) despite near-uniform tropical climate, signalling strong
delta-specific control.

\subsection{Standard validation overstates transferable skill}
A gradient-boosting model that explains 65\% of SOC variance under random $k$-fold
cross-validation ($R^2=0.65$) drops to negative skill under spatial-block
cross-validation ($R^2=-0.69$) and collapses under leave-one-delta-out
($R^2_{\text{mean}}=-5.8$; $R^2_{\text{median}}=-1.7$) (Table~\ref{tab:tiers},
Fig.~\ref{fig:tiers}). The implied \emph{transfer gap} (random minus LODO) is 6.5
$R^2$ units for the flexible model and 2.7 for the ridge baseline --- the magnitude by
which conventional validation overstates out-of-distribution skill. We report the
median LODO $R^2$ as the headline because the mean is dominated by the smallest folds
(e.g.\ Sundarbans, $n=11$, $R^2=-34$), where unconstrained extrapolation produces
extreme values; the qualitative conclusion is identical under either summary.

\subsection{Every unsampled delta lies outside the area of applicability}
For all eight held-out deltas, \emph{zero} percent of the mangrove area falls inside
the model's area of applicability (Table~\ref{tab:perdelta}, Fig.~\ref{fig:aoa}): each
delta is a separated cluster in covariate space, so every cross-delta prediction is an
extrapolation. Split-conformal intervals calibrated to 90\% marginal coverage on
held-out training residuals realise widely varying empirical coverage across deltas
(0\% for Sundarbans to 90\% for Amazon--Amap\'a; Table~\ref{tab:perdelta}), confirming
that the finite-sample coverage guarantee --- which assumes calibration and test data
are exchangeable --- breaks under cross-delta shift. The AOA result is model-agnostic
and unchanged across the climate, geomorphic, and tidal feature sets.

\subsection{Adding geomorphic and tidal forcing does not restore transfer}
A natural objection is that the failure merely reflects missing predictors. We tested
this by augmenting the climate covariates first with geomorphic position
(distance-to-coast and distance-to-river) and then with tidal forcing (mean and spring
tidal range and the tidal form factor, derived from the EOT20 ocean-tide model
\citep{hartdavis2021eot20}) --- the dominant physical control on mangrove sediment
trapping and carbon burial. The tidal covariates are physically faithful, separating
macrotidal systems (Amazon--Amap\'a, Zambezi, Rufiji; spring range $3.6$--$4.9$~m) from
microtidal ones (Musi--Banyuasin, $0.7$~m). Yet the transfer gap is essentially
unchanged across the three feature sets (gradient-boosting LODO $R^2_{\text{mean}}$ of
$-7.0$, $-5.7$ and $-5.8$ for climate, $+$geomorphic and $+$tidal respectively), and
the AOA-inside fraction remains zero for every delta throughout. The transferability
limit is therefore not an artifact of an incomplete covariate stack along the major
physical axes. Nor is the in-distribution skill an artifact of the GSOCmap prior:
removing it leaves the random-CV $R^2$ unchanged ($<0.005$) and the median LODO $R^2$
nearly so ($<0.2$; Supplementary Information), so the transfer gap does not depend on
that potentially circular feature.

\subsection{Covariate shift does not fully explain which deltas fail}
If transfer failed simply because held-out deltas were environmentally distant, the
\emph{magnitude} of covariate shift should predict the \emph{magnitude} of error. It
does not: across the eight folds, neither the mean dissimilarity index nor the
multivariate energy distance correlates with LODO error in the expected direction
(Spearman $\rho=+0.24$, $p=0.57$ for dissimilarity vs.\ $R^2$; $\rho=-0.38$, $p=0.35$
for energy distance vs.\ RMSE). With only eight folds this test is underpowered, so we
treat it as inconclusive rather than a true null; the robust observation is that
\emph{every} delta lies outside the AOA, so error is governed less by how far a delta
sits than by how the SOC--covariate relationship itself changes between deltas
(concept shift). Consistent with a physical rather than statistical origin, the
dominant shift axis for each delta is interpretable (Supplementary Information): tidal
range for the macrotidal Amazon--Amap\'a, Rufiji and Musi--Banyuasin systems,
precipitation seasonality (bio15) for the Sahelian Saloum--Gambia, and monsoon thermal
regime (bio3, bio6, bio9) for the Sundarbans.

\subsection{Aboveground biomass carbon transfers no better than soil carbon}
To test whether the transferability limit is peculiar to soil carbon, we repeated the
entire analysis for aboveground biomass carbon (AGB-C), sampling the 30~m Simard
product within each delta's mangrove mask (500 points per delta) and applying the
identical models, validation tiers, AOA and conformal machinery. The result mirrors the
SOC case (Table~\ref{tab:pooltransfer}): gradient-boosting skill of $R^2=0.64$ under
random cross-validation falls to $+0.12$ under spatial-block CV and to a median of
$-0.60$ under leave-one-delta-out, with every delta again entirely outside the AOA.
That two carbon pools with different measurement principles and spatial structure
fail transfer in the same way strengthens the conclusion that the limit is a property
of the deltas, not of one variable. We note one caveat: because the Simard biomass
product embeds a canopy-height-to-biomass relationship that itself depends on climate,
predicting AGB-C from bioclimatic covariates is partially circular, so the AGB result
is best read as a companion to, rather than an independent replicate of, the SOC
result.

\subsection{Total ecosystem carbon and the cost of mis-transfer}
Combining the pools gives the total mangrove ecosystem carbon density and stock of the
benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:totalcarbon}). Across the eight core deltas the total stock
is \SI{495}{\tera\gram} C, soil carbon constitutes 77\% of the total on average
(consistent with the belowground-dominated mangrove carbon budget), and per-hectare
densities span an order of magnitude---from \SI{106}{\mega\gram\per\hectare} (Zambezi)
to \SI{639}{\mega\gram\per\hectare} (Musi--Banyuasin). Because both pools fail
cross-delta transfer, this heterogeneity cannot be reconstructed for an unsampled
delta from a globally-trained model: applying the global model's typical bias
($\sim$100--200~Mg\,ha$^{-1}$ RMSE per pool, Table~\ref{tab:perdelta}) to a delta the
size of the Sundarbans translates into stock errors of tens of Tg C, comparable to the
stocks themselves. This quantifies the practical cost of assuming transferability in
crediting or accounting.

\section{Discussion}
Our central result is that the apparent accuracy of a global mangrove soil-carbon model
is largely an artifact of in-distribution validation. The same model that explains two
thirds of the variance under random cross-validation cannot predict an unsampled delta
better than its global mean, and--more diagnostically--every held-out delta lies wholly
outside the model's area of applicability. Because this AOA result is model-agnostic and
invariant to the addition of geomorphic and tidal predictors, it is not readily
dismissed as a quirk of one algorithm or a missing covariate. The world's major
mangrove deltas occupy separated regions of environmental space, and a model trained on
some of them is, for an unsampled delta, always extrapolating.

The practical implication is specific. Wall-to-wall blue-carbon maps are increasingly
used to set baselines for carbon crediting and restoration prioritisation, yet their
reported uncertainties derive from in-distribution validation and from conformal-style
guarantees that, as we show, fail under cross-delta shift (empirical coverage as low as
0\%). A crediting-grade soil-carbon estimate for a delta absent from the training data
therefore cannot be inherited from a global model; it requires local calibration cores.
The same logic identifies where global maps \emph{can} be trusted---inside the AOA---and
turns the area of applicability into an actionable map layer for users.

That covariate-shift magnitude does not order the per-delta failures points to concept
shift: the relationship between environment and soil-carbon density itself differs among
deltas, plausibly through delta-specific sediment supply, salinity regime and
hydroperiod that our global covariates only partially capture. The interpretable
dominant shift axes---tidal range in macrotidal deltas, precipitation seasonality in the
Sahelian Saloum, monsoon thermal regime in the Sundarbans---are consistent with a
physical rather than a sampling-coverage origin. This suggests that closing the gap will
require not more global covariates but methods that admit region-specific structure:
hierarchical or partial-pooling models, transfer learning with a few target-delta cores,
or explicit process constraints.

Several limitations qualify these conclusions. In-situ soil-carbon labels remain sparse
and unevenly distributed: some folds are small ($n=11$ for the Sundarbans), which
inflates the mean LODO error and motivates our use of the median, and the global core
distribution forced the inclusion of two estuarine systems to reach four continents.
Depth harmonisation to 0--100~cm relies on a small fraction of extrapolated and
organic-matter-inferred values. The AOA threshold is a provisional quantile rather than
a tuned boxplot-whisker rule, and tidal forcing is taken from an empirical tide model
rather than a delta-resolving hydrodynamic simulation. The aboveground-biomass analysis
inherits the Simard product's structure and its partial climate circularity, and
because the AGB and SOC label sets are not co-located, total carbon is combined from
per-delta pool summaries rather than per-pixel sums.

These limitations also define the path forward. The framework extends naturally to
historical extent-change emissions and to sea-level-rise scenarios; co-located
field plots would allow per-pixel total-carbon mapping with jointly propagated
uncertainty; and because every delta is specified only by a bounding box, the entire
pipeline is portable to any coastal system. The contribution of this work is less a new
map than a measurement of how far existing maps can be trusted beyond where they were
trained---and a reusable, uncertainty-aware benchmark for improving that reach.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Study systems and the delta registry}
Candidate deltas were drawn from the major mangrove systems of the global tropics and
defined by generous geographic windows. To avoid post-hoc selection, inclusion rules
were fixed before any modelling: a system enters the benchmark as a soil-carbon
leave-one-delta-out (LODO) fold if (i) its mangrove area exceeds a minimum threshold,
(ii) it carries at least five usable in-situ soil cores, and (iii) windows do not
overlap, so no pixel is shared between a training and a held-out delta. We further
required the fold set to span at least four continents so that the out-of-distribution
test is environmentally diverse. Mangrove extent was taken from the Global Mangrove
Watch v3 (2020) product \citep{bunting2022gmw3}; each window's extent was clipped and
its area computed in the World Cylindrical Equal Area projection (EPSG:6933) so that
area-by-density stock accounting is unbiased. Systems passing the area but not the
label criterion were retained as prediction-only deltas. Two estuarine systems
(Everglades and an Australian estuary) were added to secure North American and
Oceanian coverage; the final v1 soil-carbon benchmark comprises eight core deltas
across four continents (Table~\ref{tab:deltas}). The complete criteria, thresholds,
and a change log are given in the Supplementary Information.

\subsection{In-situ carbon labels}
Soil organic carbon labels were derived from the Coastal Carbon Network Data Library
v1.6.0 \citep{ccn2024}, retaining cores classified as mangrove habitat with valid
coordinates. For each core we integrated the measured depth series to a standardized
0--100~cm areal stock,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SOC}_{0\text{--}100} \;=\; 100 \sum_i \mathrm{BD}_i \, f_{C,i}\, \Delta z_i
\qquad [\mathrm{Mg\,C\,ha^{-1}}],
\label{eq:soc}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{BD}_i$ is dry bulk density (g\,cm$^{-3}$), $f_{C,i}$ the organic carbon
mass fraction, $\Delta z_i$ the thickness (cm) of layer $i$ truncated at 100~cm, and
the factor 100 converts g\,cm$^{-2}$ to Mg\,ha$^{-1}$. Where carbon fraction was
unreported we estimated it from loss-on-ignition organic matter using the median
$f_C/f_{OM}$ ratio of $0.427$ computed over $\sim$12{,}000 paired CCN samples. Layers
with physically implausible values were dropped (bulk density outside
$0.02$--$1.8$~g\,cm$^{-3}$, carbon fraction outside $0.001$--$0.60$). Cores were
required to reach at least 80~cm; the remaining gap to 100~cm was filled with the
deepest valid layer's carbon density and flagged. After a 0.5/99.5 percentile trim for
label hygiene, the procedure yielded 2{,}489 mangrove SOC cores (median
278~Mg\,ha$^{-1}$; 4\% extrapolated, 7\% with carbon inferred from organic matter).
Aboveground biomass labels were obtained from the 30~m Simard et al.\ product
\citep{simard2019,simard2019data} and converted to carbon with a factor of 0.47; AGB
labels were acquired for all eight core deltas and reserved for the total-carbon
analysis.

\subsection{Covariates}
Predictors spanned the major physical controls on mangrove carbon: bioclimate (CHELSA
v2.1 bio1--19; \citealp{karger2017chelsa}), a soil-carbon prior and soil context
(GSOCmap; SoilGrids bulk density and CEC; \citealp{poggio2021soilgrids}), land cover
(Copernicus Global Land Service), geomorphic position (distance to coast and to major
rivers, computed from Natural Earth in equal-area projection), and tidal forcing (mean
and spring tidal range and the tidal form factor, derived from the M2, S2, K1 and O1
constituents of the EOT20 ocean-tide model; \citealp{hartdavis2021eot20}). Gridded
driver layers were sampled at each core in the layer's native coordinate reference
system, with nodata handled per layer; the external driver archive is referenced
through a configurable path (\texttt{MDBC\_LAKE}) and not redistributed. The GSOCmap
prior is itself a soil-carbon model and is reported but interpreted cautiously to avoid
circularity. Some archived layers were corrupt or unreadable (SoilGrids texture,
OpenLandMap, fine terrain); the sampler tolerates and logs these, and they are excluded
from the v1 stack.

\subsection{Models and three-tier validation}
We modelled $\log(1+\mathrm{SOC})$ with two estimators of contrasting flexibility: a
ridge-regression baseline (standardized, median-imputed features) and histogram
gradient-boosted regression trees. Each was evaluated under three nested validation
schemes. Tier~1 is standard random five-fold cross-validation. Tier~2 is spatial-block
cross-validation \citep{roberts2017}, in which whole $5^\circ$ blocks are held out to
suppress the spatial-autocorrelation leakage that inflates random validation
\citep{ploton2020}. Tier~3 is leave-one-delta-out: the model is trained on all but one
delta and evaluated on the held-out delta, the operational analogue of predicting an
unsampled system. We summarise the transfer gap as the Tier~1 minus Tier~3 skill, and
report both mean and median LODO $R^2$ together with RMSE and bias back-transformed to
Mg\,ha$^{-1}$.

\subsection{Area of applicability}
For each held-out delta we computed the area of applicability following
\citet{meyer2021}: features are standardized and weighted by model importance, and the
dissimilarity index (DI) of each test point is its importance-weighted distance to the
nearest training point, normalised by the mean nearest-neighbour distance within the
training data. The applicability threshold is set from the (outlier-aware) upper
quantile of training DI values, and we report the fraction of each held-out delta
falling below it (inside the AOA).

\subsection{Conformal prediction intervals}
To attach calibrated uncertainty we used split-conformal prediction. A calibration
subset (20\%, minimum 50 cores) was withheld from each LODO training set; the
half-width of the prediction interval was taken as the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of the
absolute calibration residuals with $\alpha=0.1$, giving 90\% marginal coverage under
exchangeability. We report the empirical coverage realised on each held-out delta as a
direct test of whether that exchangeability assumption survives cross-delta shift.

\subsection{Transfer diagnostic}
To test whether the magnitude of covariate shift explains which deltas fail, we
quantified each held-out delta's distance from the training feature space two ways: the
mean importance-weighted dissimilarity index used for the AOA, and the multivariate
energy distance between the delta and the pooled remaining deltas on standardized
features (subsampled for tractability). We correlated each shift measure with the
corresponding LODO error (Spearman $\rho$) and, to name the dominant axis of difference
for each delta, ranked covariates by the standardized mean difference between the delta
and the rest. Given only eight folds, these correlations are reported as descriptive
with explicit acknowledgement of low statistical power.
