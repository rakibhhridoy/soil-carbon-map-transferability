\section{Delta selection and the frozen benchmark}
Candidate deltas were drawn from the major mangrove systems of the global tropics and
defined by generous geographic windows (bounding boxes). Inclusion rules were fixed
before any modelling. A system enters the benchmark as a soil-carbon leave-one-delta-out
(LODO) fold if (C1) its mangrove area within the window, computed from Global Mangrove
Watch v3 (2020) in the World Cylindrical Equal Area projection (EPSG:6933), is at least
40~km$^2$; (C2) it represents a coherent deltaic or estuarine mangrove setting; (C3) it
carries at least five \emph{usable} mangrove soil cores, cores that pass the
harmonization in Section~\ref{si:soc} rather than merely falling in the window; (C4) the
fold set spans at least four continents; and (C5) windows do not overlap, so no pixel is
shared between a training and a held-out delta. Systems passing the area but not the
label criterion were retained as prediction-only deltas; the remainder were excluded.

A key design choice is that C3 is evaluated on \emph{usable} cores, not the raw in-window
count: many windows contain marsh, seagrass or shallow cores that do not yield a
0--100~cm mangrove SOC stock. This distinction is large (Table~\ref{tab:registry}): for
example the Everglades window holds 276 raw cores but only 7 usable mangrove SOC cores,
and Zambezi 280 versus 12. The first registry pass yielded only three continents, failing
C4; we therefore added two estuarine systems with ample usable cores: the Everglades (North America) and an Australian estuary (Oceania). The Australian fold was subsequently
excluded because its mangrove area (18~km$^2$) fell below the C1 floor, and no large
Australian or Papua New Guinean delta carried usable deep SOC cores in the source
database. The final v1 soil-carbon benchmark therefore comprises eight core deltas across
four continents; Oceania remains a documented data gap.

\section{SOC label harmonization}
\label{si:soc}
Soil cores from the Coastal Carbon Network Data Library v1.6.0 classified as mangrove
habitat with valid coordinates were integrated to a standardized 0--100~cm areal carbon
stock by Eq.~\ref{eq:soc-si}, where $\mathrm{BD}_i$ is dry bulk density
(g\,cm$^{-3}$), $f_{C,i}$ the organic carbon mass fraction, and $\Delta z_i$ the layer
thickness (cm) truncated at 100~cm.
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SOC}_{0\text{--}100} = 100 \sum_i \mathrm{BD}_i\, f_{C,i}\, \Delta z_i,
\qquad f_{C,i} = \begin{cases} f_{C,i}^{\text{meas}} & \text{if measured} \\
0.427\, f_{OM,i} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\label{eq:soc-si}
\end{equation}
Where carbon fraction was unreported it was estimated from loss-on-ignition organic
matter using the factor 0.427, the median $f_C/f_{OM}$ ratio across $\sim$12{,}000
paired CCN samples. Layers with physically implausible values were removed (bulk density
outside $0.02$--$1.8$~g\,cm$^{-3}$ or carbon fraction outside $0.001$--$0.60$). Cores
were required to reach at least 80~cm; the residual gap to 100~cm was filled with the
deepest valid layer's carbon density and flagged as extrapolated. After a 0.5/99.5
percentile trim, the procedure produced 2{,}489 usable mangrove SOC cores (median
278~Mg\,ha$^{-1}$, range 15--844~Mg\,ha$^{-1}$; 4\% of cores extrapolated to 100~cm and
7\% with carbon inferred from organic matter).

\section{Covariate stack}
Each covariate was sampled at every label point in the layer's native coordinate
reference system; nodata values were masked per layer, and tidal constituents, which are masked on land, were sampled by nearest valid ocean cell within a small search
radius. Climate is represented by the 19 CHELSA v2.1 bioclimatic variables; a global
soil-carbon prior by GSOCmap; soil context by SoilGrids bulk density and cation-exchange
capacity; land cover by the Copernicus Global Land Service 2015 classification;
geomorphic position by distance-to-coast and distance-to-river computed from Natural
Earth in equal-area projection; and tidal forcing by the mean and spring tidal range and
the tidal form factor derived from the M2, S2, K1 and O1 constituents of the EOT20 ocean
tide model. Several archived driver layers in the source data lake were corrupt or truncated on read (SoilGrids texture, OpenLandMap soil-carbon, and the fine terrain and hydrology tiles) and were excluded; the sampler tolerates and logs such failures rather
than aborting. GSOCmap is itself a soil-carbon model, so it is retained but treated
cautiously to avoid circularity; a sensitivity analysis removing it is reported in
Section~\ref{si:gsoc}. Coverage (fraction of cores with a finite value) is given in
Table~\ref{tab:covcoverage}.

\section{Validation, AOA and conformal details}
Tier~1 is standard random five-fold cross-validation. Tier~2 is spatial-block
cross-validation: points are assigned to $5^{\circ}$ latitude--longitude blocks and whole
blocks are held out across five folds, so that training and validation points are
spatially separated and the autocorrelation leakage that inflates random validation is
suppressed. Tier~3 is leave-one-delta-out: one delta is held out entirely while the model
trains on all others; because windows are non-overlapping (criterion C5) no pixel is
shared, eliminating leakage between train and test deltas. The area of applicability is
computed by standardizing features, weighting them by model importance, and taking each
test point's importance-weighted distance to its nearest training point normalised by the
mean within-training nearest-neighbour distance; the applicability threshold is the
outlier-aware upper quantile of training dissimilarity values, and we report the fraction
of each held-out delta below it. Split-conformal intervals withhold a calibration subset
(20\%, minimum 50 cores) from each LODO training set and set the interval half-width to
the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of absolute calibration residuals with $\alpha=0.1$; we report
the empirical coverage realised on each held-out delta.

\section{Is the zero AOA coverage an artifact?}
\label{si:aoa}
A potential concern is that every held-out delta falls outside the area of applicability
simply because leaving one delta out leaves few environmental clusters in the training
set, making any held-out group look novel. We tested this by computing the AOA-inside
fraction of held-out cores under three holdout designs of increasing separation
(Table~\ref{tab:aoavalidity}). Random cores drawn from across the training deltas land
overwhelmingly inside the AOA (82\%), confirming that the threshold is not pathologically
small; spatial-block and whole-delta holdouts both give 0\%. The applicability threshold
thus discriminates familiar from novel environment as intended, and the zero coverage of
held-out deltas reflects genuine separation in covariate space.

The zero coverage is also robust to the threshold magnitude. Recomputing the per-delta
AOA-inside fraction while scaling the boxplot-whisker threshold from $0.5\times$ to
$2\times$ its standard value leaves the median at zero throughout, and even at twice the
standard threshold no delta exceeds zero percent inside. The held-out deltas are far
enough outside the training distribution that no reasonable threshold places them inside,
so the result is a property of the data rather than of the threshold rule.

\section{GSOC prior sensitivity}
\label{si:gsoc}
Because GSOCmap is itself a soil-carbon model, its inclusion as a covariate could inflate
in-distribution skill. Removing it changes the gradient-boosting random-CV $R^2$ by less
than $0.005$ and the median LODO $R^2$ by less than $0.2$ (Table~\ref{tab:gsoc}), and the
transfer gap is essentially unchanged. The transferability failure is therefore not an
artifact of the prior.

\section{Transfer diagnostic}
For each held-out delta we measured covariate shift two ways: the mean
importance-weighted dissimilarity index used for the AOA, and the multivariate energy
distance between the delta and the pooled remaining deltas on standardized features
(subsampled to at most 400 points per side for tractability). Each shift measure was
correlated with the corresponding LODO error across the eight folds (Spearman $\rho$).
Across folds, neither correlation reached significance and both carried the unexpected
sign (dissimilarity vs.\ $R^2$: $\rho=+0.19$, $p=0.65$; energy distance vs.\ RMSE:
$\rho=-0.38$, $p=0.35$); with only eight folds the test is underpowered, so this is
reported as inconclusive rather than a null result. To name the dominant axis of
difference for each delta we ranked covariates by the standardized mean difference
between the delta and the remaining deltas. These axes are physically interpretable:
tidal range for the macrotidal Amazon--Amap\'a, Rufiji and Musi--Banyuasin; precipitation
seasonality (bio15) for the Sahelian Saloum--Gambia; and monsoon thermal regime (bio3,
bio6, bio9) for the Sundarbans.

\paragraph{Direct test of concept shift.} To show that the environment--carbon
relationship itself differs between deltas (not merely the mean level), we fit a linear
model of SOC on four interpretable standardized covariates (spring tidal range, mean
annual temperature, annual precipitation, distance-to-coast) for the five deltas with at
least 30 cores. A specification with shared slopes and per-delta intercepts (permitting a
level offset only) achieved a median within-delta cross-validated $R^2$ of 0.43; allowing
per-delta slopes raised this to 0.67. The fitted standardized slopes
(Table~\ref{tab:conceptshift}) flip sign between deltas: for instance, the SOC response
to mean annual temperature is positive in Amazon--Amap\'a and Rufiji but strongly negative in the Mekong, direct evidence that the relationship, not just the level, is
delta-specific.

\section{Aboveground biomass and total ecosystem carbon}
Aboveground biomass carbon labels were generated by sampling the 30~m Simard et al.\
(2019) mangrove biomass product within each delta's mangrove mask (the product is masked
to mangrove extent, so positive-valued pixels are mangrove), drawing 500 random valid
pixels per delta and converting biomass to carbon with a factor of 0.47. These points

\section{Reproducibility}
The full pipeline is released with a pinned software environment
(\texttt{requirements.txt}) and a single-command runner (\texttt{run\_all.sh}) that
executes data acquisition through analysis in order. Primary datasets are downloaded by
versioned acquisition scripts that write per-source provenance manifests recording source
URLs, file sizes and checksums; the external climate and soil driver archive is referenced
through a configurable path (\texttt{MDBC\_LAKE}) and not redistributed. Random seeds are
fixed, and the frozen leave-one-delta-out delta registry is released with the benchmark so
that the splits, figures and every reported number regenerate deterministically. All
manuscript figures and numerical tables are produced directly from the results files by a
single script, eliminating hand-transcribed values.
